# Assignment practice and mock examinations

The default Question Bank screen is a saved library: Grade 6–12 → subject → dated generation batch. Each grade collection shows stored, approved, draft and published counts; batch cards open review or one shared publishing form. Publishing from a batch selects approved questions only within that grade, subject and batch, across all pages. The audience selector supports independent learners, eligible subscribed schools or both. Generating for another grade uses that grade’s reviewed outcomes and adds new saved batches without replacing earlier questions. The generation workspace and library link to one another; content jobs can be filtered by grade. The library refreshes periodically so completed background generation becomes visible. Its API returns grouped counts and batch dates, never answer keys or question text. The existing database stores the questions and stable batch numbers; there is no browser-only archive or destructive migration in this update. Deploy readiness now checks for the library/publication capabilities and numbered-batch schema before publishing the matching frontend.

Every generation run is stored as a numbered batch with its original generation date. The 15-question worker chunks and retries share one durable number; later runs receive a new number. A migration groups existing chunk IDs into dated runs without deleting or regenerating questions. Numbers are permanent identifiers and are not reused after deletion. The question-review screen opens on the latest saved batch; the Generation batch selector retrieves earlier batches or explicitly shows all saved batches. Batch-scoped approval and publication retain that filter across every page, and publishing multiple batches still creates distinct assignments per generation run, subject and topic. Dates display in Africa/Nairobi time.

The Select all approved questions action stays in the Question Bank header on every tab. Selecting all approved questions opens the Questions tab and shows the audience selector and Publish content button above the question list.

The Question Bank’s **Select all approved questions** action clears batch/grade/subject/topic filters, selects a saved snapshot across every page, and exposes the publishing audience controls. Individual questions can be excluded before publishing; draft and rejected items are never included.

Approval records quality review; it does not deliver new assignments. Platform Admin explicitly publishes approved items to individual learners, eligible subscribed schools, selected schools, or both audiences. Publication creates real assignments in each destination school, grouped by grade → subject → topic. Draft/rejected items stay private. Topic batches use the profile grade and up to 30 questions, resume unfinished work and retain unseen-first/cohort-overlap rotation. A topic with fewer than 30 approved items can start without waiting for unrelated topics. Bank activation plus an active subscription gates school access; independent learners retain the existing account activation gates. Completed practice reviews remain accessible from their topic.

Previously saved practice uses its own assignment route. It remains available for resuming/review, but approval alone cannot open a new unpublished practice pool. New published work uses the existing native assignment and submission workflow, including normal essay marking. Equal-mark pools above 30 use per-student variants; mixed-mark selections split into batches of at most 30. Publishing the same bank items to the same audience again skips existing published copies, while newly subscribed/activated schools can receive them through a later publish action. Publishing captures the currently eligible school audience; it does not silently add future schools. Publication runs as a durable content job, prioritised ahead of AI generation; partial failures can be retried without duplicating completed assignments. No parent SMS is sent by publishing.

Legacy practice uses its own assignment route. Existing records retain source PRACTICE for compatibility, and are excluded from mock history and unfinished-mock limits. Multiple choice, true/false, numeric and short-answer practice use their appropriate controls. Essays save a full response and expose model-answer feedback on completion; they are explicitly self-review and are not automatically scored as essays.

New junior Mathematics mocks contain 40 numbered questions and 100 marks: Section A has 20 multiple-choice items worth one mark each; Section B has 20 structured questions, each with four one-mark sub-parts and saved working. Marks target Measurement 32, Geometry 24, Number 21, Algebra 14 and Data/Probability 9. For Grade 9 revision, the source-grade allocation is 18/32/50 marks for Grades 7/8/9; younger papers stay in their selected grade. The weights supplied by the proprietor are a platform practice blueprint, not a verified official KNEC specification. Free-form working is retained but does not receive inferred method marks: each explicit sub-part is marked separately.

English and Kiswahili junior papers use original platform-authored dialogue, cloze, reading comprehension, informational text, poetry and language-use sections. Shared stimuli remain with consecutive questions. The Grade 6 JESMA sample informs organisation only; no JESMA passages are copied. The 40-question platform language papers default to 80 minutes; Mathematics defaults to 120 minutes. A 70-minute timed drill is also offered. Context templates can recur across generated versions.

Other bank-based mocks need at least 40 approved questions across three topics. Short senior-topic starters are displayed as unavailable full papers, rather than relabelled as examinations. A reviewed Mathematics bank can supply a full paper when it has 20 choice and 20 numeric/short-answer items across three topics; these are weighted to 20/80 marks, with explicit answer-based marking rather than inferred method marks. Reviewed language banks must supply all six passage/language sections before appearing as full-paper options. These rules apply to senior grades too; incomplete pools remain assignment practice. This does not assert that an arbitrary reviewed bank matches an official subject specification or that every senior-school subject already has a full paper.

The server enforces deadlines and validates each answer against the stored question type. Structured answer keys and keyword rules are not released before submission. Final marks and explanations use the stored snapshot; late changes are ignored. Submitted mocks receive an explicitly estimated practice performance level: EE1 ≥90%, EE2 ≥75%, ME1 ≥58%, ME2 ≥41%, AE1 ≥31%, AE2 ≥21%, BE1 ≥11%, otherwise BE2. These are revision estimates, not official KNEC results. Historical choice-only papers keep their existing marking semantics. Exam JSON parsing is scoped to 512 KiB so saved workings fit without changing authentication or signed-webhook parsing.

Language mock presentation follows the supplied paper's page layout: a paper masthead, shared dialogue with aligned speaker names and numbered blanks, then all related numbered questions and lettered A–D options together. Page navigation moves between shared stimuli rather than hiding a dialogue behind individual questions. Local cloze blank labels are displayed using the paper's global question numbers without changing stored answers. Reviewed HTML passages continue through the existing sanitizer. Existing saved papers without passage data are retained and display a link to create a new passage-based paper; missing dialogue is never invented for an old snapshot.


Generation failures retain actionable provider guidance in the saved job error. DeepSeek HTTP 402 is reported as INSUFFICIENT_BALANCE; HTTP 400/404/422 as INVALID_REQUEST (check the configured model/request); credential rejection as NOT_CONFIGURED; rate limits and temporary/network errors remain distinct. Balance/configuration failures require administrator action and are not represented as problems that a short wait will fix. Provider diagnostics remain in backend logs; public errors use fixed guidance. Historical UNKNOWN messages are not rewritten or guessed: after deployment, retry one saved failed job to obtain its current result before retrying a whole collection. This diagnostic change does not top up an account, alter production credentials/models, or claim to resolve an unobserved provider failure.

### Released assignment review

The assignment player renders `ReleasedReview` only after the existing submission reports `resultsReleasedAt` and the released-results endpoint supplies answer-level data. Unreleased results remain pending and do not fetch the review payload. The review uses a single numbered navigator (collapsible on phones), neutral/amber incorrect states and compact answer rows. Official partial marks and unscored states remain visible.

Known generic feedback is filtered by exact normalized strings; custom notes and actionable numeric guidance remain. Explanations support safe inline bold markers and paragraphs. A stored trailing `Transfer check:`, `Check your understanding:`, `Try this:` or `Retrieval cue:` becomes a separate practice prompt. Only an explicit supplied `Answer:` is revealed as its answer; otherwise the learner can compare reasoning with the original explanation. No answer is invented.

“Hide answers & retry” and transfer responses are local, ungraded practice state. They make no submission, grading or AI calls and do not change the saved result. Navigating to another question resets that question's practice state. Existing timed assessment, draft-saving and release rules remain in the assignment player.


### Fresh topic sets

New bank topic publications require at least 20 approved questions per saved batch, grade, subject and topic. Short selections remain stored with an actionable publication issue. Generation now targets 30 questions per selected outcome; outcomes with fewer than 20 saved questions remain eligible for a top-up.

After submission, Start next set opens a private set of 20–30 unseen questions from the learner's published grade/subject/topic pool. Existing practice history and questions delivered through fixed assignment variants are excluded. Existing drafts resume; prior results and pending teacher reviews remain unchanged. Allocation balances pools where possible: 40 unseen questions produce two sets of 20.

When fewer than 20 unseen questions remain, more content must be generated, reviewed and published by an administrator. No duplicate padding, silent repeat batches or paid generation on learner login/completion occurs. Existing short assignments and snapshots remain preserved. School subscription and bank access requirements still apply.
