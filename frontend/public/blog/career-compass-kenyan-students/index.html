Career Compass: How to Choose the Right Grade 10 Pathway for Your Child

A parent's guide to balancing your child's dreams, your family's reality, and the Kenyan job market.

Assignment Hub Team · Updated 2026-11-15

Key takeaways

  • Align three perspectives: the Student’s Dream, the Parent’s Hope and Kenyan Reality.
  • Family circumstances and sibling expectations deserve discussion; neither should decide a child’s future.
  • Plan for formal work and informal enterprise: combine a qualification with demonstrable practical skills.

The short answer

A career pathway should not be a guessing game. Start a structured conversation about what your child loves, what your family can afford, and what Kenyan education and work opportunities require; Career Compass helps organise that conversation.

What the Research Says: Birth Order and Social Class

Rohrer, Egloff and Schmukle (2015) found no lasting birth-order effects on traits such as extraversion or conscientiousness across US, UK and German panels. Their study examined personality, not which career an individual should choose.

OECD’s PISA 2018 analysis connects socio-economic background with educational opportunities and expectations. Treat money, time, information and family networks as planning conditions: ask what support could widen the options, rather than equating family income with ability or ambition.

  • Rohrer, Egloff & Schmukle (2015), PNAS
  • OECD (2019), PISA 2018 Results, Volume II

The three layers of Career Compass

01

Student’s Dream

Ask, “What work would you do for free?” as a way to discover interests, not to recommend unpaid work; then ask which activity they willingly practise without marks or rewards.

Compare favourite subjects, hobbies and two pieces of work they are proud of. An interest becomes stronger evidence when the learner keeps improving after feedback, so try a small project before dismissing a creative or technical ambition as unrealistic.

02

Parent’s Hope

Name what sits behind your preference: security, reliable income, professional recognition or a fear of unemployment. Share those hopes honestly, while leaving room for your child’s interests and a route you can both support.

Write an affordable annual budget covering fees, transport, accommodation, devices and materials. Compare public and private options, investigate funding, and keep both university and TVET routes open until you have checked the requirements and total costs.

03

Kenyan Reality

Kenya’s informal economy makes practical capability important alongside qualifications, but informal work can also mean unstable income and limited protection. Use current KNBS employment tables to examine the market; a statistic about new jobs is not the same as the share of all workers.

Explore university, accredited TVET, apprenticeships, freelancing and enterprise without promising easy earnings. A hybrid route could combine IT training with a freelance portfolio, or an agriculture qualification with experience in a local enterprise.

Choose subjects before choosing a job title

A broad pathway label is not enough: compare the actual three-subject combinations and the schools offering them. Our portal-supplied catalogue lists ST2056 as Agriculture, Aviation and Computer Studies, and SS1040 as Computer Studies, Fasihi ya Kiswahili and Indigenous Language.

Confirm the current codes, availability and selection rules on the MoE portal with your school. Then check the intended institution’s subject and grade requirements; a planning recommendation is not an admission or placement guarantee.

How to choose without guessing

Check each step as a family; use the list to guide a conversation, not to score your child.

How This Connects to Your Child’s Career

Leave the conversation with two possible routes, one budget and one next action. Revisit the evidence each term: a stronger plan can preserve options while turning a distant ambition into a manageable step.
