Key takeaways

  • Read EE, ME, AE and BE alongside the assessed skill and teacher’s comment.
  • Find one missing step before buying more revision materials.
  • Review progress calmly; one report is not a career prediction.

The short answer

EE means Exceeding Expectation; ME, Meeting Expectation; AE, Approaching Expectation; and BE, Below Expectation. Read each level alongside the task and teacher’s comment.

What the Research Says

PISA 2018 links socio-economic background with educational opportunities and expectations. It does not establish how Kenyan parents interpret AE or BE: ask about each family’s resources rather than assuming fear or low ambition.

  • OECD (2019), PISA 2018 Results, Volume II

What EE, ME, AE and BE actually tell you

CBC performance descriptions summarise evidence from an assessment: Exceeding Expectation, Meeting Expectation, Approaching Expectation and Below Expectation. Before reacting to a letter, establish which task was assessed, what successful performance looked like and whether the learner had an opportunity to demonstrate the skill.

A learner may meet the expectation when calculating an answer but need support when explaining the method. Read the task and teacher’s comment together instead of treating a subject’s overall descriptor as a complete description of everything your child knows.

Use a small practice-and-review cycle

Select one skill for the next week and ask your child to attempt a short question without notes. Check the explanation together, write one correction in the learner’s own words and revisit a similar question after a gap; the aim is to see whether the correction can be used independently.

For fractions, ask the learner to draw the same whole before comparing two values, then move to symbols only when the drawing makes sense. Our Grade 7 fractions guide shows how to identify a missing step instead of repeating a rule that has not been understood.

  1. Choose one missing skill with the teacher.
  2. Attempt a related question independently.
  3. Explain the first correction.
  4. Retry after a gap without hints.
  5. Review the evidence together next week.

Watch patterns, not one disappointing afternoon

Keep a simple record with the date, skill practised, kind of error and what changed on the next attempt. A mark that rises only when a parent gives hints tells a different story from a learner who can explain and apply the method a week later.

If difficulties appear suddenly across several subjects, discuss attendance, workload, language demands and wellbeing with the school. Persistent problems deserve a teacher conversation; adding more worksheets without understanding the pattern can use family time and money without addressing the cause.

  • Notice repeated misconceptions rather than isolated slips.
  • Share two examples of work when asking for school support.
  • Agree when the teacher and family will review the plan.

Make support fit your household

A useful learning routine does not require a private study room, a new device or paid tuition. A borrowed book, a short quiet interval and a parent asking the learner to explain one step can create a repeatable routine; choose arrangements your household can sustain.

Explain any limits honestly to the teacher, including shared devices, data costs or a parent’s work schedule. Ask which practice can be done on paper and which feedback matters most, so limited resources are directed at a specific need rather than a long list of activities.

Use digital feedback as evidence for the next lesson

When using Assignment Hub, start with published work appropriate to the learner’s grade and subjects, then read the feedback after an independent attempt. Treat instant marking as information for the next practice session, not a verdict on intelligence or a replacement for the teacher’s formal assessment.

A report can help identify a revision priority, but it does not automatically establish KCBE eligibility or university admission. For older learners, connect the pattern with the Career Compass conversation and check the school’s current subject and assessment guidance before making pathway decisions.

Questions parents ask

What does EE mean?

EE means Exceeding Expectation. Ask what evidence shows the learner applying the skill beyond the expected level; it is not a claim that every part of the subject is mastered.

Is BE a failing grade?

BE means Below Expectation for the assessed work. It identifies a need for support, not a permanent label or a complete decision about promotion or a career.

How can I help my child improve?

Ask for one specific missing skill, practise a small example, discuss the correction and retry later. Share repeated difficulties with the teacher.
