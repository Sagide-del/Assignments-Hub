STEM, Arts, or Social Sciences? A Parent's Guide to Senior School Pathways

By Assignment Hub Team · 2026-11-10

Senior school · 3 minute read

A choice that should start with listening

Senior school pathway conversations can feel high stakes, especially as families hear different advice from friends, social media and tuition centres. The best starting point is not a fashionable career title. It is a learner’s evidence: subjects they enjoy, skills they sustain when work is difficult, practical activities they return to, teacher observations and the kind of problems they like solving. Kenya’s senior school framework groups options into three broad pathways: STEM, Social Sciences, and Arts and Sports Science. Schools may offer different combinations, so families should check the official placement information and the school’s available tracks before making a decision.

The STEM pathway

STEM brings together science, technology, engineering and mathematics. It includes areas such as pure sciences, applied sciences, technical studies and career and technical studies. It can suit learners who enjoy investigating how things work, modelling, measuring, coding, designing or solving multi-step problems. It is not reserved for children who score perfectly. A learner who is willing to practise foundations, ask questions and persist through unfamiliar problems can grow strongly in STEM. At home, notice whether your child enjoys experiments, construction, computing, agriculture technology, health sciences or explaining why a result changed.

The Social Sciences pathway

Social Sciences helps learners explore people, communities, business, governance, languages and the systems that shape daily life. It may connect naturally with interests in entrepreneurship, communication, law, education, public service, hospitality, social research and related careers. A strong fit is often visible in a learner who reads broadly, asks thoughtful questions about society, communicates clearly, notices evidence and enjoys working with people. This pathway is not an easier alternative to STEM. It has its own demanding literacy, analytical and project skills.

The Arts and Sports Science pathway

Arts and Sports Science develops creative practice, performance, design, media, physical education and sport-related strengths. It can support futures in the creative economy, coaching, design, media, performance, wellness and other specialist fields. Parents sometimes mistake a creative interest for a hobby that should be left outside school. In fact, disciplined artistic and sporting work builds observation, collaboration, resilience, portfolio development and communication. Look for evidence of consistent practice, curiosity, feedback-taking and commitment, not only a single performance or competition.

How to choose without guessing

Create a one-page pathway conversation. List three subjects or activities that energise the learner, three that drain them, two recent pieces of work they are proud of and two skills that need support. Then ask: What work would they willingly improve at? What environments help them concentrate? Which teachers can give examples of their strengths? Compare these answers with actual school options and entry guidance. Visit open days where possible. A pathway should be a direction with room to grow, not a lifelong label fixed by one examination.

Build evidence through practice

Use low-pressure practice to test interest. A learner considering STEM can try a science investigation, a maths problem set or a coding lesson. A learner considering Social Sciences can analyse an article, plan a small survey or present a local issue. A learner drawn to Arts can create a short portfolio, performance reflection or design brief.
